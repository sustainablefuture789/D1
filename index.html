What happens to a wave

Energy hitting a surface can go three ways

A radio or radar wave that meets a material is partly reflected, partly absorbed and turned into heat, and partly transmitted through. Engineering a material means controlling that split.

EMI shielding

Protect the electronics

EMI shielding reduces unwanted electromagnetic energy reaching sensitive electronics.

SE (dB) = 10 log₁₀ (Pincident / Ptransmitted)

10 dB stops 90% of the power, 20 dB stops 99%, 30 dB stops 99.9%. Real shielding comes from reflection, absorption and multiple internal reflections. Higher reflection is not automatically better: the reflected energy can interfere with nearby systems.

Radar-absorbing material

Control the echo

A radar-absorbing material is designed to reduce the energy returned toward the radar by increasing absorption and controlling electromagnetic impedance.

Bhavira material has the potential to reduce electromagnetic signature under specific conditions. It does not make a platform invisible or undetectable, and no stealth performance is claimed.

The Bhavira platform

Tunable polymer–graphite composite

Graphite flakes dispersed in a polymer. Once enough flakes touch, they form a conductive network, and the network's density and geometry shape how the composite responds to electromagnetic waves.

Polymer matrix+Graphite filler+Microstructure+Layer thickness=Electromagnetic response

What we have measured so far

60:40 graphite:polymer, 0.5 mm thick, rectangular waveguide, X band 8.2–12.4 GHz, three samples.

ReflectedAbsorbedTransmittedrange of 3 samples

These initial measurements indicate significant reflection and measurable absorption. At this composition and thickness, the material behaves more like a reflective EMI-shielding material than a strongly absorbing RAM.

Further controlled measurements are required to optimise absorption. Small negative absorption values near 11.3–12 GHz point to calibration or fixture ripple, which the next test round should remove.

Why multilayer materials?

Layers give more control over where the wave goes

A multilayer architecture can provide additional control over impedance matching, internal reflections and electromagnetic loss: an outer layer eases the wave in, inner layers absorb, a conductive interface or substrate turns back what is left for a second pass.
